One recorded call becomes structured record updates, owner-tagged follow-ups, and a one-minute channel brief. Every item traces to the words that created it. A person approves anything a customer could ever see.

The full method is below. You already record the calls. The recordings answer to nobody, and a buyer said it best.

How it works

01Capture the call

Any recorder that produces a transcript works. Gemini meeting notes, Zoom transcripts, Fireflies.

Raw speaker-labeled transcripts produce the best extractions. Notetaker summaries still work, and the workflow lowers its confidence scores to match, because secondhand notes are already an interpretation.

The baseline this replaces is familiar. Updating records after each conversation takes a rep about thirty minutes per client, so the records go stale and deal awareness lives in whoever happened to be in the room.

02Extract with receipts

No quote, no item. The workflow extracts what was said. It never invents. Every item carries the verbatim words that created it, so anyone can audit any entry back to the source in one click.

Talk the meeting explicitly deferred becomes a follow-up, never a task. Here is the shape of one extracted item.

{
  "title": "Send pricing summary to their CFO",
  "owner": "us",
  "estimate": "S",
  "route": "committed",
  "tier": "review",
  "source_quote": "Can you get the pricing
    summary over to our CFO by Friday?"
}

Watch for the item you cannot quote. Cut it. A phantom commitment costs more than a missed one.

03Route what was said

Meetings wander. People riff on things they want someday. The workflow routes that talk instead of promoting it, using the language rather than the topic.

  • Committed. Decision language with an owner. Becomes a work item this cycle.
  • New ask. A real request outside agreed scope. Logged and sized, presented as a proposal.
  • Exploration. Musing with no owner and no date. Goes to the parking lot.
  • Resurrected non-goal. Something already scoped out. Flagged so a person re-decides.

The tell is an owner and a yes. Enthusiasm alone routes down. When a route is unclear, route down and let a person promote it.

The parking lot is expansion signal. Raising it back later ("you mentioned this twice, want us to scope it?") is where future work comes from.

04Fork by owner

Owner is the first fork. Items owned by your side are candidates for execution. Items owned by the other side are tracked as waiting-on entries and never executed.

That single rule keeps the workflow from quietly doing work that belongs on someone else's plate, and it lets tomorrow's brief say "waiting on Dana since the 14th."

05Apply the tier rules

Rules decide the tier. The stricter rule always wins. Reversible, scoped, testable work is eligible to run unattended. Everything else waits for review.

Deterministic overrides sit above the model's proposal, so a misclassification can never grant itself more autonomy. Large items wait. Low-confidence items wait.

And anything a customer or prospect will see waits for a person. Always, regardless of size.

Watch for the tier assigned by vibes. If you cannot name the rule that decided it, the rule layer is missing.

06File and brief

Approved output lands where your team already works. Record updates drafted into the CRM. Tasks with an owner, a size, and the source quote. A channel brief a teammate reads in a minute.

When the meeting updates prior work, the workflow matches the existing record instead of creating a duplicate.

The front end is a door, and you should name your own. Slack teams get a Slack brief, Microsoft teams get Teams.

07Graduate autonomy

Supervised first. Graduation is measured, never assumed. At the start, a person reviews every extraction before anything writes to a live system.

Score each pass on four failure modes. Invented items, missed asks, wrong owners, wrong tiers. The workflow graduates to writing on its own only after consecutive clean passes, and only for the tiers the rules already allow.

Trust in this design is a property you measure, with a date attached.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the workflow cost to run?

Processing a call costs cents in model usage, and spend only happens on calls a person chose to process. The stack is what you already run. A recorder, a language model with an API key, your CRM or task system, and the channel your team reads.

Does the workflow write to the CRM automatically?

At the start, nothing writes without review. Record updates are drafted, a person approves them, and the approval takes seconds. After consecutive clean review passes, the tiers the rules allow can graduate to writing unattended. Customer-facing content keeps its human gate permanently.

How is this different from an AI notetaker summary?

A summary describes the meeting. This workflow files it. Items land in your CRM and task system with an owner, a size, a route, and the verbatim quote that created them, and a person gates anything consequential.

What happens to ideas discussed but not committed?

They route to a parking lot instead of the task queue. Each entry keeps who raised it and how seriously it was discussed. Reviewed monthly, the parking lot becomes expansion conversations, because a topic a client raised twice is a topic worth scoping.

How long does setup take?

The kit's extraction prompt and routing rubric run on day one against your first transcript. The supervised review period typically runs a few weeks, and its length is decided by your own clean-pass count rather than a calendar. Graduation is earned on evidence.

Can the extraction be trusted on messy, wandering calls?

Wandering calls are the design case. The routing layer exists because meetings drift, and the quote requirement means drift never silently becomes commitments. Ambiguous language routes down for a person to promote, so the failure mode is a question rather than a phantom task.
