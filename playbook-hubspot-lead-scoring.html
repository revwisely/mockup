The HubSpot Lead Scoring Playbook

Top-scored leads that never buy? Build fit and engagement scores from your closed deals, name what each grade triggers, and test the score before anything routes on it.

The full method is below. Fit rules and engagement points earned from the deals you won and lost, a HubSpot score built beside the old one, an action for every grade, and two tests that read it as a chance and a range.

A score can be calculated exactly as designed and still rank the wrong people first.

The failure this playbook fixes

How it works

01Pull the deals you won and lost

Export closed-won and closed-lost deals with each associated company, 12 to 24 months back. Sort them by close date and set the newest third aside as test deals before you read anything. Every rule and every point comes from the oldest two thirds, the build deals.

For each build deal, record its size, industry and region, the title of the person who signed or led it, and the lost reason.

Your closed deals show who actually buys. Set them beside the profile in your strategy deck, and let the backtest in step 06 pick. Rules written from the deals that test them would pass whether or not they describe your buyers, which is why the test deals stay unread.

02Write fit rules a stranger can check

"Mid-market and growing" is an opinion. "Employee count between 100 and 500, checked on the LinkedIn company page" is a rule. Write each rule so two people applying it get the same answer, and note how many of your wins match it.

Schools, government bodies and competitors get large negative points, so no amount of engagement lifts them into a top grade. Then list the fit properties that must be filled before a score is trusted.

03Earn engagement points from what came before wins

For each event, count how often it happened in the 90 days before a build deal was created, on wins and on losses. An event earns points when it shows up more often before wins.

A pricing page view that appears on 70% of wins and 70% of losses tells you someone was shopping, and little about who buys.

Cap each group so one noisy event cannot fill the score, and set decay to match your sales cycle. HubSpot offers decay intervals of 1, 3, 6 or 12 months.

Watch for the busiest records in the portal. A student, a job seeker or a competitor can out-click a real buyer, and without the disqualifiers they rise to the top.

04Build it in HubSpot, beside the old score

HubSpot's lead scoring tool sits under Marketing, then Lead Scoring, on Marketing Hub or Sales Hub at Professional or Enterprise. It allows more than one score per object, so the new model runs beside the old one until the backtest reads.

Build it as a combined score. Grades run from A1 to C3. The letter is fit, from A for high fit, and the number is engagement.

HubSpot's test record and distribution preview confirm the arithmetic. Whether the score ranks buyers is the job of step 06.

05Name the action for every grade

A1 and A2 go to a rep within a set number of days. A3 is the right company gone quiet, so it goes to nurture and gets reviewed monthly. B1 gets a rep review before any outreach.

C1 is very active at a company that cannot buy, so it gets the resource it asked for and no sales outreach.

A blank field and a failed rule both add zero. A record that does not meet a rule gets 0 points, and a blank property does not meet a rule that looks for a value, unless you score blank on purpose. So records missing a required fit property go to a review list, where a person looks it up, instead of to a grade.

Watch for a threshold with no action attached. When the customer definition changes, it keeps sorting accounts the old way.

06Backtest the fit letter before anything routes

Grade the test deals, the newest third that no rule was written from, and compare the share of wins and of losses that land in A. Read the fit letter, since a won customer keeps engaging after the deal and inflates the number on every win. Read fit as it stood at the close where HubSpot's property history allows, or flag the deals whose size or industry has changed since.

Sign the pass line before you look. Ours is a 90% probability, worked out from the counts, that won deals land in A at a higher rate than lost deals.

With 40 wins and 60 losses, 28 of 40 against 24 of 60 is a 99.8% chance, with the real gap somewhere from 13 to 44 points. 24 of 40 against 33 of 60 is 69%, too close to call, so rewrite the fit rules from the build deals and read again on deals that close after the rewrite.

07Read opportunities by grade every month

Credit each opportunity to the grade the account held on the first of the month. A meeting raises engagement, so crediting the new grade would let the outcome promote the account after the fact.

Compare A1 and A2 against A3 and B. One month is often too few accounts to call, so pool the months until the chance passes the line.

No difference after three pooled months sends you back to the fit rules. A review list that grows every month means fix the data before the weights.

Score from the deals you closed, and let those deals grade the score.
