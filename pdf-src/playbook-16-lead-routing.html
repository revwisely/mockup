The method

How It Works

Six steps. The first four are set up once, the fifth runs every business day and the sixth every week. The worksheets for every one of them ship as the markdown files in this kit.

01List every way a lead comes in

Routing rules only act on leads that reach them. List every source, including demo forms, resource forms, chat, meeting bookings, product sign-ups, shared inboxes, event lists and referrals. For each one, write where the lead is first counted, how it travels to the CRM hop by hop, and who owns that connection.

Watch for a source with no count of its own. It cannot be checked, so fix that before writing a rule.

02Score fit and intent apart

Fit scores who the lead is. Intent scores what they did. The two are never added into one number. A signal you could not verify scores null, never zero, and two or more nulls on the fit side send the lead to a person. Every lead gets a one-screen brief with the source of each fact.

03Write the routes and the assignment order

Qualified gets a named owner, a dated next step and an alert with the brief. Nurture gets marketing emails. Monitor is watched and never sold to. No fit gets what they asked for and nothing else. Assignment rules are checked in order and the first match wins. If an AI model helps, it proposes a score, and the rules set the route and the owner.

Watch for the last rule. Name a fallback owner for the lead that matches nothing, or it gets no owner and no alert.

04Send the close calls to a person

Review is checked first, before any band. Conflicting evidence, an unclear duplicate, a score near a band edge and an identity that cannot be verified go to a person with the reason attached. A lane runs unattended only after a streak of confirmed routes, and one changed route sets the streak back to zero.

05Check every day that each lead landed

A routing failure rarely shows an error. Count the leads where they were first recorded, count them again in the CRM, and compare. Every lead in the gap gets a name and a reason before the read is closed. A source that normally sends leads and sent none gets a test submission the same day.

Watch for a check that starts from the CRM's own count. That compares the CRM with itself.

Where a lead stopsWhat it looks likeHow to see it
1 At the sourceThe form tool holds the submission or files it as spamRead the source's own log and its held folder
2 Between toolsThe connector reports success and the CRM has no recordCompare the source count with the CRM count
3 In the CRM, no routeThe record exists and no rule matched itList yesterday's records with no route value
4 Routed, no ownerA route is set and the owner is blank or inactiveList routed records with no active owner
5 Owned, untouchedAn owner is set and nothing happened inside the limitList qualified records past the limit with no activity

06Read weekly whether routing is working

Write down what "working" means before the first read. Each week, read time from arrival to first touch, the share of qualified leads touched inside the limit, leads with no active owner, the age of the review queue and the routes a person changed, lane by lane. Once a month, join each route to what happened next. Rules change at this read and nowhere else.

Three rules for the daily read

  • Count at the source first. The CRM's count only covers the records it holds.
  • Read the held folder every time. A real lead filed as spam exists at the source and is absent everywhere else.
  • A zero is a finding until explained. Write down each source's normal range so a zero stands out.
