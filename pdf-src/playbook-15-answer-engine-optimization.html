The method

How It Works

Six steps, set up once and read every two weeks. The worksheets for every one of them ship as the markdown files in this kit.

01Pull the questions buyers ask AI

An answer engine names a company in reply to a question, so the unit of work is the question. Pull from at least three sources, such as AI question data with monthly volumes, sales calls, buyer communities, Search Console and your support inbox. Group the phrasings, add the volumes, and tag each group as buyer, learner, job seeker, tool shopper or other meaning.

Watch for the highest-volume row. It is often a tool shopper or a job seeker question, and neither turns into a call.

02Read who gets cited, and sort open from owned

Ask each question on at least two engines, three times each, in a fresh session. Log the companies named, the domains cited, the page type and the shape of the answer. Sort each question as open, contested, owned or wrong shape. When a question is owned, add a platform, a company size or a task and read the narrow version.

03Check what you have already published

Search your own live site for each question and grade what comes back as exact, adjacent or none. Record it per surface in one ledger, with the next asset for each row.

Watch for a ledger that is checked against itself. Read the live site every time.

04Pick three levers the research supports

The kit grades seven levers that reach a small firm and lists eight tactics the research does not support. Choose at most three for the first eight weeks. More than that and you cannot tell which one moved the read.

magnetiz.ai03

05Write one answer per question

Put the question in a heading in the asker's words, and answer it in the first two sentences. Use specific numbers with their source and date, name the tools and the fields, date the page, and serve it as plain HTML. Cover what the cited pages leave out.

06Measure the share of answers that name you

Freeze the questions as prompts and write down what "it worked" means before the baseline. Run each prompt ten times on each engine in a fresh session, then again every two weeks. Count a win only when the answer names you. We run this read on ourselves. Our baseline on October 30, 2026 was zero, with Magnetiz named in none of 799 answers across 27 questions and four engines.

LeverWhat the research found
L1 Narrow questionsA brand-new name reached 66.4% of answers to a narrow question and 15.8% to a broader one (Ahrefs, 2026)
L2 Service page per offerFor agency questions, service pages were the most cited first-party page type at 30.4% (Ahrefs, 750 prompts)
L3 Third-party listsLists of "best X" were 43.8% of cited page types, and 35% sat on low-authority domains (Ahrefs)
L4 Long-form articlesLong-form articles were 50 to 66% of cited LinkedIn content (Semrush with LinkedIn data, 89,000 URLs)
L5 Mentions, breadth firstMentions in videos and on other sites were the strongest correlates across 75,000 brands (Ahrefs)
L6 Numbers and datesStatistics and quotations raised a source's share in a 2023 lab test (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024)
L7 Share of appearancesThe same list of brands came back under 1% of the time across 2,961 runs (SparkToro and Gumshoe)

What the research does not support

  • llms.txt. No correlation with citations across about 300,000 domains (SE Ranking).
  • Schema added for AI. 1,885 pages against matched controls, no lift (Ahrefs, 2026).
  • A rank in an AI answer. Order repeated under 0.1% of the time (SparkToro and Gumshoe).
