The method

How It Works

Seven steps, four to six weeks per test. The worksheets for every one of them ship as the markdown files in this kit.

01Shortlist two or three signals

Few signals, each one visible from outside the account. Rate every candidate on whether you can see it before the conversation, how many teams already act on it, and what checking it costs. Most teams chase the same few signals, like content engagement and website visits. Put at least one on the list that fewer teams watch.

Watch for signals you can only see on the call. Those are discovery questions and stay off the list.

02Write the fact that must still be true

One card per signal. The event you see, the one fact that would make it meaningless if it stopped being true, where to check that fact, how long the signal stays fresh, the opening line it makes possible, and the rule for when it retires.

03Split the list and sign the rule

The signal arm gets accounts that show the signal. The comparison arm gets accounts from the same band that do not, with your usual message. Keep the sender, channel, days and follow-ups the same. Plan on 100 accounts per arm, and sign how sure you need to be to keep the signal. We use a 90% chance that the signal arm really replies more.

Watch for calling it early. A small test can still be read, it just gives a wide range, and a lucky week read as a sure thing is how a hunch becomes a strategy.

04Check every fact before every batch

Check each account's fact at its source. Send only when it is still true and inside its fresh window, and hold the account when the answer is "cannot tell". Then read the rendered message the tool will actually send. When we read every message before one load, 8 of 129 contacts weren't employees of the company the message was written for.

05Send both arms and count

Replies per account contacted and meetings booked, for each arm. Keep the pre-send tallies too, because they show how often the signal goes stale or cannot be checked.

06Read it against the signed rule

Look the counts up and report the chance and the range together. The range is where the real gap probably sits.

07Scale what earned its place, test the next

A kept signal scales with the pre-send check still on every batch and gets read again in a quarter, because a signal that works today can stop working once more teams act on it. A retired signal makes room for the next one on the shortlist.

The terms, as this kit uses them

  • Signal. An event you can see from outside the account that might mean it is ready to buy.
  • The fact. What must still be true for the signal to mean anything.
  • Fresh window. How long after the event the signal still counts.
  • Comparison arm. Accounts from the same band without the signal, sent your usual message.
  • The chance that counts. How sure the read must be that the signal arm really replies more before the signal is kept.
