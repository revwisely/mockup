The method

How It Works

Seven steps, built once and read every month. The worksheets for every one of them ship as the markdown files in this kit.

01Pull the deals you won and lost

Export closed-won and closed-lost deals from the last 12 months, with each associated company. Record its size, industry and region, the title of the person who signed or led it, and the lost reason. Your closed deals show who actually buys. Set them beside the profile in your strategy deck, and let the backtest pick.

02Write fit rules a stranger can check

"Employee count between 100 and 500, checked on the LinkedIn company page" is a rule. Write each one so two people get the same answer. Schools, government bodies and competitors get large negative points, so no amount of engagement lifts them into a top grade. List the fit properties that must be filled before a score is trusted.

03Earn engagement points from what came before wins

For each event, count how often it happened in the 90 days before a deal was created, on wins and on losses. An event earns points when it shows up more often before wins. Cap each group, and set decay to match your sales cycle. HubSpot offers 1, 3, 6 or 12 months.

Watch for the busiest records in the portal. A student, a job seeker or a competitor can out-click a real buyer, and without the disqualifiers they rise to the top.

04Build it in HubSpot, beside the old score

The lead scoring tool sits under Marketing, then Lead Scoring, on Marketing Hub or Sales Hub at Professional or Enterprise. It allows more than one score per object, so the new model runs beside the old one until the backtest reads. Build a combined score. Grades run from A1 to C3, the letter is fit and the number is engagement. HubSpot's test record and distribution preview confirm the arithmetic.

05Name the action for every grade

A1 and A2 go to a rep within a set number of days. A3 goes to nurture, reviewed monthly. B1 gets a rep review before any outreach. C grades get no sales outreach, with the reason recorded. HubSpot points cannot tell a blank field apart from a failed rule, so records missing a required fit property go to a review list for a person.

Watch for a threshold with no action attached. When the customer definition changes, it keeps sorting accounts the old way.

06Backtest the fit letter before anything routes

Grade last year's wins and losses and compare the share of each that lands in A. Read the fit letter, since a won customer keeps engaging after the deal. Sign how sure you need to be first. We use a 90% chance.

07Read opportunities by grade every month

Credit each opportunity to the grade the account held on the first of the month, and compare A1 and A2 against A3 and B. Pool the months until the chance passes the line.

The terms, as this kit uses them

  • Fit. Whether the company and the person could buy, from properties like size, industry and title.
  • Engagement. Recent activity, from events like a demo request or a pricing page view.
  • Grade. HubSpot's combined score, A1 to C3. The letter is fit, the number is engagement.
  • Review list. Records missing a required fit property, looked up by a person before they get a grade.
