The method

How It Works

Seven steps. The worksheets for every one of them ship as the markdown files in this kit.

01Harvest artifacts, not adjectives

A guideline says confident but approachable. An exemplar set shows it. Collect what worked and what missed, each with one line on why, plus the lines customers quoted back to you. The rejected drafts matter as much as the published ones; the losers teach where the line is.

Watch for the harvest that skips call recordings. Your customers' own words are the best voice material you own, and they are already recorded.

02Split mechanical from judgment

Could a person with no taste check this rule correctly, every time, with no argument? Yes means mechanical, and it belongs in a linter. No means judgment, and it belongs in the rubric and the exemplars. Most voice documents mix the two and enforce neither.

03Build the linter

The mechanical half becomes a check that runs before a person reads a word. It reports the exact line and the exact rule, exits non-zero on error, and runs on outside drafts the moment they arrive.

Watch for the linter getting credit for quality. A clean lint means the tells are gone, nothing more.

04Write the rubric, and run it three times

The judge scores what a linter cannot hold. Predictability, earned claim, register, voice match, contribution. Blind, because a judge told the founder wrote it scores the founder.

Run it three times and take the majority. We measured this in our own system. The same draft, the same rubric, the same model, scored clean and slop in consecutive runs, four of nine clean on an unchanged piece. Trust a reproduced finding over the verdict label.

05Calibrate against known-good and known-bad

Five pieces you know are good, five you know are bad, two your team argued about, answers kept out of the judge's reach, pass bars written down first. If the judge cannot separate your known-good from your known-bad, the rubric is wrong, not the drafts. A judge that fails a known-good is enforcing something your voice does not believe, and left alone it flattens the writing.

06Distribute one version

A layer that lives in one person's cloud and gets downloaded, edited, and re-uploaded has as many versions as it has readers. One source, one named owner, dated versions, retirements recorded loudly. Anyone outside your core team is the test case, and the brief should be a link.

07Close the loop

What ships goes back in. A piece that landed becomes an exemplar, a piece that missed becomes a known-bad, and a correction you have made three times becomes a rule. A context layer that never grows is a document again, and documents are what sent the drafts back needing you.

The Calibration Gate

CheckStarting barYour result
Known-good pieces passing5 of 5
Known-bad pieces failing5 of 5
Borderline verdicts stable across three runs1 of 2

Write your bars down before the first run. A bar set afterward equals whatever the run achieved. A judge that passes a known-bad is missing an axis; name it and add it.
