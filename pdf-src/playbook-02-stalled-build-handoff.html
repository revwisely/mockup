The method

How It Works

Seven steps. The worksheets for every one of them ship as the markdown files in this kit.

01Audit what exists

List the stalled build honestly, in three columns. What still runs. What runs with a human hand on it. What broke and when. Most stalled builds are closer to alive than anyone remembers.

Watch for the audit that turns into a rebuild pitch. Reviving beats rebuilding until proven otherwise.

02Name the owner

One person, with hours in numbers. How many per week, and what moves off their plate to make room. A manager who cannot name what moves has not decided. No named owner means the revival does not start, and the audit asks the question in its first row.

03Define runnable

Write the acceptance bar before touching anything. The owner launches unassisted, knows what good output looks like, recovers the likely failures with the runbook alone, and finds out from the system when it goes quiet. Capture baselines now; the after columns fill 30 days post-handoff, on the calendar. When the work had stopped entirely, the baseline is the cost of not doing it.

04Pick the doorbell

The front end is a door, never the house. Slack teams get a Slack front end. Microsoft teams get Teams. The requirement is that anyone can run it, without watering down the power of what it is doing.

Watch for a front end that asks your team to learn a new tool. The door they already open is the door.

magnetiz.ai03

05Wrap, don't rebuild

Put a thin, runnable layer over what exists. The smallest reliable slice ships first, gets used, and earns the next slice. Every change stays reversible. The stalled build is the asset, and wrapping preserves it while a rebuild would gamble it.

06Transfer ownership

The runbook lives where the work happens, and the training does too. The owner comments on output in plain language, the system files the note as a lesson and confirms it back in the thread, and a wrong lesson rolls back in one step. The kit ships the mechanism (corrections-to-lessons.md). No prompt engineering is asked of anyone.

07Prove it

Six checks, each with a date, then a cadence. Owner runs it unassisted. Output passes, in their words. A failure recovered by runbook alone. Silence simulated, reported by the system. The backup runs it end to end. A correction filed and confirmed as a lesson. Then it re-proves quarterly or on owner change, because a handoff is a state, never a ceremony.

The Proving Run, All Six

CheckDate
Owner ran the workflow end to end, unassisted, from the doorbell
Output passed, and the owner said why in their own words
One failure simulated; owner recovered it with the runbook alone
Silence simulated; the owner found out from the system
The backup ran it end to end, unassisted
A correction filed as a comment; the lesson confirmed in the thread

Re-prove the first check every quarter, or on any owner or backup change, whichever comes first. The date goes in the runbook history.
