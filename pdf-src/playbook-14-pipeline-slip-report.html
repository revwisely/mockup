The method

How It Works

Seven steps, set up once, run every week and checked every quarter. The worksheets for every one of them ship as the markdown files in this kit.

01Set your lines from the deals you won

Pull won deals from the last four quarters and work out the average days each one spent in each stage. A deal past 1.5 times that average is stuck. Write down the titles that sign at your buyers, so everyone flags the same deals.

02Find the evidence your CRM already keeps

Stage dates, last and next activity, close date history, contact roles and logged meetings. HubSpot and Salesforce both keep most of it. Turn on close date history today, because every week without it is a week of pushes you cannot count.

Watch for a field that exists and is mostly empty. A rule that reads a blank field flags nothing and looks clean.

03Run seven rules on every Commit and Best Case deal

Each week, check every deal closing this quarter. Flag the ones stuck in stage, quiet for more than seven days with nothing booked, already pushed once, missing the person who signs, resting on one buyer contact, with no critical event, or with no known path to a signature.

04Ask one question per flag, a day before the call

Send the report to the deal owners the day before the forecast call, flagged deals first. Each flag carries one question about what happened on the buyer's side. A flag is a question, never a verdict. The owner's answer, with evidence, decides.

magnetiz.ai03

05Move the deals the evidence does not hold up

One flag, the deal stays and the owner answers. Two or more flags, it moves from Commit to Best Case unless evidence clears a flag. A push with no signer in the deal is Best Case until the signer joins a conversation.

06Freeze a snapshot in week one

Export every deal closing this quarter with its category and flags, then leave it untouched. A snapshot edited later will agree with the outcome whether or not the forecast was any good.

07Score the quarter against the snapshot

Compare what each category promised with what closed, and what flagged deals did against unflagged ones. A rule earns its place when the deals it flags close less often. After two quarters, your own record replaces the research below.

What the rules can and cannot tell you

  • Correlation. Deals that go quiet lose more often. The rule shows where to look, and the owner's answer shows why.
  • Vendor research. Ebsta and Gong publish their samples, and not their full methods.
  • Your motion. Your own quarters outrank any benchmark. That is what step 07 is for.
