The method

How It Works

Six steps. The first three are set up once, the fourth and fifth run on every brief and the sixth every week. The worksheets for every one of them ship as the markdown files in this kit.

01Pick the decision and write the questions

A model asked to research a company returns a company profile, and most of a profile changes nothing a seller does. Pick the one decision the brief serves. Then write six to eight questions that a public page could answer, that ask for a fact, and whose answer could change the decision. Anything only the account can answer goes on a list for the call.

Watch for "are they a good fit". That is a judgment, and a person makes it from the facts.

02Rank the sources that may be evidence

Primary is the account saying it about itself, on its website, careers page, job posts, help center, newsroom and filings. Secondary is someone else saying it, and a seller treats it as a lead to confirm. Write down what is ruled out, including a search snippet for a page never opened and the model's own memory. Save the full text of every page the run opens.

03Set what a claim has to carry

Every line is one fact with a citation. A citation is the address of a page fetched in this run, the exact words that support the line, and the date. Each line is labelled as a number, a stated fact or a stated reason, and as primary or secondary. A stated reason needs the reason in the quote.

Watch for two true facts joined with "because". That is a read, and reads go in their own section.

04Check every citation before a seller reads it

The model that wrote a line is the worst judge of whether it is supported. The check compares text with text, so a person with the page open or a short program can do it. One bad citation refuses the whole line, and nothing is dropped. Claims plus refusals has to equal the number of lines proposed.

What refuses a lineWhat it catchesHow to see it
1 No citationA fact the model produced from memoryThe line carries no page and no quote
2 Quote too shortOne or two words that appear on almost any pageCount the characters, and require 20 or more
3 Page never fetchedA link the model invented or rememberedLook for the address in the list of pages the run saved
4 Quote not on the pageA real page credited with words it never saidSearch the saved page text for the whole quote

05Hand over a one-page brief that shows its refusals

The brief has fixed sections. What the pages say, each claim with its quote. What we think it means, each read naming the claims it rests on. What we could not support, with the reason. What was not found. Questions for the call. Refusals stay on the page so the seller does not repeat them.

Watch for acting on an old brief. Re-open the page behind a claim before a message goes out about it.

Section of the briefWhat it holdsWho may write it
What the pages sayClaims that passed, each with its quote, page and dateThe model, through the check
What we think it meansReads, each naming the claims it rests onA person, or the model in this section only
What we could not supportRefused lines, each with the reasonThe check
Not foundQuestions no page answered, with the pages triedThe model
Questions for the callWhat only the account can answerYou, once

06Read weekly whether the research is right and used

The check proves a page said something. It cannot prove the claim follows from the quote. Each week, read the share of lines refused and why, open ten claims that passed and judge each one, and count what sellers marked as wrong or out of date after their calls. Read less only after weeks with no wrong claim.

Three rules for the check

  • No model does the checking. Asked whether a quote is on a page, a model will sometimes say yes when it is close.
  • Test the check before it guards anything. Feed it lines you made up, and confirm it refuses the bad ones.
  • A refused share of zero is a finding. Confirm the check still runs before you believe it.
