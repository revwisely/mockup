Does the page your AI research cites really say that? Have every fact in an account brief carry an exact quote from a page that was opened, and print what could not be supported.

The full method is below. One decision and the questions behind it, sources ranked before research starts, a rule for what a claim has to carry, a check on every citation, a one-page brief that shows its refusals, and a weekly read of whether the research is right and used.

The brief had a source link. The page behind it said something else, and the seller found out on the call.

The failure this playbook fixes

How it works

01Pick the decision and write the questions

A model asked to research a company returns a company profile, and most of a profile changes nothing a seller does. Pick the one decision the brief serves. Should we work this account, what should the first message be about, what do I need before this call, or who else has to be in the deal.

Then write six to eight questions. Each one has to be answerable from a public page, ask for a fact, and have an answer that could change the decision. Anything only the account can answer goes on a list for the call.

Watch for "are they a good fit". That is a judgment, and a person makes it from the facts.

02Rank the sources that may be evidence

A model cites whatever it finds first. Sort sources into two tiers before research starts, so the tier travels with every claim.

  • Primary, the account saying it about itself. Its website, careers page, job posts, help center, newsroom and filings
  • Secondary, someone else saying it. News coverage, review sites and data vendor profiles. A seller treats these as a lead to confirm

Write down what is ruled out, including a search snippet for a page that was never opened and the model's own memory of the company. Set how old each kind of fact can be before it has to be checked again.

Save the full text of every page the run opens, with its address and the time. The check in step 4 needs it.

03Set what a claim has to carry

Every line the model hands back is one fact with a citation. A citation is the address of a page fetched in this run, the exact words on that page that support the line, and the date. A link says where to look. A quote says what was found, and a quote can be checked.

Each line is labelled as a number, a stated fact or a stated reason, and as primary or secondary. A stated reason needs the reason in the quote. Two true facts joined with "because" is a read, and reads go in their own section of the brief.

The kit's prompt asks for exactly this, and tells the model to say "not found" before it answers from memory.

04Check every citation before a seller reads it

The model that wrote a line is the worst judge of whether it is supported. The check compares text with text, so a person with the page open or a short program can do it. Four things refuse a line.

  • No citation, a fact the model produced from memory
  • A quote under 20 characters, one or two words that appear on almost any page
  • The page was never fetched, a link the model invented or remembered
  • The quote is not on the page, a real page credited with words it never said

One bad citation refuses the whole line. And nothing is dropped, so claims plus refusals has to equal the number of lines proposed.

Watch for a check done by a model. Asked whether a quote is on a page, a model will sometimes say yes when it is close.

Our own research agent runs this check in code on every brief it writes.

05Hand over a one-page brief that shows its refusals

The brief has fixed sections. What the pages say, each claim with its quote. What we think it means, each read naming the claims it rests on. What we could not support, with the reason. What was not found. Questions for the call.

Refusals stay on the page so the seller does not repeat them on a call. A brief with four claims and three refusals is an honest brief about a thin account.

Watch for acting on an old brief. Re-open the page behind a claim before a message goes out about it. A role that was open on Monday can be filled by Friday.

06Read weekly whether the research is right and used

The check proves a page said something. It cannot prove the claim follows from the quote, that the page is right, or that anyone read the brief. So each week, read the share of lines refused and why, open ten claims that passed and judge whether each quote supports its claim, and count what sellers marked as wrong or out of date after their calls.

A refused share of zero is a finding. Feed the check a line you made up and confirm it still refuses.

Start with a person reading every brief. Read less only after weeks with no wrong claim, and go back to reading everything when the model, the prompt or the page fetcher changes.

Every claim with its quote, and every refusal in plain sight.
